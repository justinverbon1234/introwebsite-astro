Instagram’s AI labels are meant to show users when artificial intelligence has been used to generate or alter the content they are seeing. But recently, the system has once again shown that it is far from reliable. You may think this sounds familiar, because it is.

Back in 2024, [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2024/7/1/24190026/meta-instagram-facebook-made-with-ai-info-label-metadata) reported that photographers were already complaining about real photographs being labelled as AI after relatively minor edits. The issue became significant enough that Meta eventually changed its “Made with AI” label to the more ambiguous “AI info.” Two years later, many of the same problems are still here.

The issue has now resurfaced. Users have reported real photographs being marked as “AI Content” after relatively ordinary edits. At the same time, images that were entirely generated using AI have managed to appear without any label at all.

This creates a strange situation where the label starts to lose its meaning. If a photograph taken with a real camera can receive the same warning as an image generated entirely from a prompt.

One example of this appeared under a post from [About Face Beauty](https://www.instagram.com/p/DcHC1fEmk82/?carousel_share_child_media_id=3965149526192402682_39293096813&comment_id=18052312760797092), where a user asked, “Why is it labeled AI?” Their social media manager responded: “This photo was taken on my iPhone and then slightly edited by myself in the photo app. Our brand uses real artists and real people to create everything you see across all our platforms.”

So, if even real images can get flagged as AI, what is the label telling us. I mean seriously, what are they scanning for? It’s obviously important to try and be transparent with your users, but if 2 years later you are basically at the same place as you were before it might be time to go back to the drawing board.
