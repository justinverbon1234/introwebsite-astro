I originally just wanted a place where I could show some of the things I make.

But pretty quickly I realised that I did not really want a traditional portfolio.

Most portfolios have the same kind of structure. A big introduction, a grid of projects, an About page and then some contact information at the bottom. There is nothing wrong with that, but it did not really feel like something I would enjoy making or updating.

I wanted the website itself to be part of the work.

The idea became to create a personal space around the things I am interested in. Art, fashion, technology, design, photography and whatever else I happen to be obsessed with at the time.

That also meant I did not really want to limit myself too much from the beginning.

A lot of the visual inspiration came from magazines and fashion editorials rather than other portfolio websites. I liked the way editorial design uses really large typography next to tiny pieces of information, strange spacing, asymmetrical layouts and sometimes almost uncomfortable amounts of empty space.

So I started experimenting with those things on the website.

Typography became one of the biggest parts of the design. The huge titles, the small metadata, the volume numbers and the way things are positioned are all doing a lot of the visual work.

I actually prefer that over filling every section with images or cards.

> “Design is the method of putting form and content together.”
>
> — Paul Rand

The website also changed quite a bit while I was building it.

At first it was just HTML and CSS with a little bit of JavaScript. The Journal was completely static as well, which meant every article had its own HTML page and I had to manually update everything whenever I added something new.

That worked perfectly fine with three articles.

It would probably become incredibly annoying with twenty.

So while working on the site I started experimenting with Astro. The website is still static, but now things like the navigation, layouts and Journal pages can be reused instead of copied over and over again.

The Journal is probably the best example of that.

I can now write something in a Markdown file, give it a title, category and date, and the website automatically adds it to the archive and creates the actual page for it.

That was not really part of the original plan, but I like that the project keeps changing whenever I run into something that could be done better.

And I think that is what I want this website to be anyway.

I do not really see it as something that I will eventually finish and then leave alone. I want to keep changing things, adding experiments, writing about things I am learning and probably redesigning parts of it whenever my taste changes.

It is basically becoming an archive of what I am interested in and what I am learning at that point in time.

Which feels a lot more personal to me than just having a page with finished projects on it.
