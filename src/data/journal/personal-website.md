I wanted to build something that felt more personal than a traditional portfolio. Instead of simply showing finished work, the website became a place where I could experiment with the way I actually like to design.

The project started with a fairly simple idea: create a personal website around the things I am interested in. Art, fashion, technology, design, and whatever else I happen to be exploring at the time.

I quickly realised that I did not want it to look like a typical portfolio. I was much more interested in the visual language of magazines, fashion editorials, and printed design than rows of project cards and standard portfolio sections.

> The website should feel like an extension of my taste, not just a place where my work happens to live.

That changed the way I approached the design. Typography became one of the main visual elements. Large headlines, tiny pieces of metadata, asymmetrical compositions, and generous negative space started becoming part of the site's visual system.

The website is still evolving. That is intentional. Rather than treating it as something that eventually becomes completely finished, I want it to change as my interests, skills, and taste change with it.
